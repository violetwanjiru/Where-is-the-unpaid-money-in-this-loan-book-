Is loan delinquency concentrated or widespread, and where should collections focus first?

Concentrated: 60 of 1,868 loans hold almost all the unpaid money. The rest of the book repays.

A SQL analysis of 1,868 digital loans to 840 customers, funded 30 March to 30 May 2026. Amounts are in shillings.

The decision this supports Credit and Operations should work the 60 loans with Late status and leave lending to everyone else unchanged: those 60 loans, 3.2% of the book, hold 1,566,600.51 of the 1,566,602.78 shillings unpaid, while the 1,808 Paid loans hold 2.27 shillings between them, and the ten largest borrowers have repaid every loan they took. PAR30+ here is a proxy built on original principal and recorded days late, so read it as the size of the problem, not a precise balance. Operations should confirm outstanding balances on the 60 loans, and Credit should name the per-customer limit so a capped pilot for proven borrowers can be approved, before the next portfolio review.

Disbursements: April and May are the comparable months

April to May grew 0.95%. March holds only 56 loans because the data starts on 30 March, so April’s +1,856.56% is a base effect, not growth.

Repayment: most loans are paid, but the ratio overstates recovery

Repaid money is 1.04 times the money lent. It exceeds 1 because repayments include interest and fees, so it is not a recovery rate. Of 1,868 loans, 1,808 are Paid and 60 are Late.

Delinquency: the 31+ day bucket holds the unpaid money

Buckets use recorded days late. The 31+ bucket holds 72 loans, but 12 of them are already settled: days late on a settled loan records how late the payment was, not a live arrear.

Customers who borrowed again returned after 26.41 days on average. The window is short, so longer gaps cannot appear and the true figure is likely higher. The ten largest borrowers took 55 loans worth 5,572,200 shillings (8.04% of principal). None has a Late loan or any shortfall.

When customers were lent to again, was the previous loan unpaid?

Across 1,028 re-lending events, none followed a loan that is still unpaid. The data has no payment dates, so payment before re-lending is confirmed only where the previous loan was paid on time.

Two caveats on reading this data

Several fields are recorded, not measured at a date we choose. Days late, shortfall and status come as at an extract date the file does not state, and repaid amounts include interest, fees and penalties. There are no payment dates or outstanding balances, so PAR30+ is a proxy.

The window is short and partly incomplete. The data runs from 30 March to 30 May, and March is not comparable to later months. About two months cannot show seasonality, long repayment cycles or late-maturing defaults. A customer’s first loan in the window is not necessarily their first ever.
